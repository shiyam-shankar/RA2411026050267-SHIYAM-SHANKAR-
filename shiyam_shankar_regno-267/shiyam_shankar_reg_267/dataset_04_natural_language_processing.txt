Natural language processing, or NLP, is a multidisciplinary field at the intersection of computer science, artificial intelligence, and linguistics. It focuses on the interactions between computers and human languages, specifically how to program computers to process, analyze, and understand large amounts of natural language data. Since human language is inherently ambiguous, unstructured, and diverse, natural language processing presents some of the most challenging problems in artificial intelligence.

Typical tasks in natural language processing include tokenization, part-of-speech tagging, named entity recognition, and syntactic parsing. Tokenization involves splitting a continuous stream of text into individual words or tokens, which is a fundamental preprocessing step in any information retrieval or NLP pipeline. More complex applications of NLP include sentiment analysis, machine translation, and text summarization. For example, sentiment analysis is widely used by businesses to monitor customer feedback on social media, while machine translation systems translate text from one language to another automatically.

In modern systems, traditional rule-based NLP techniques have largely been replaced by machine learning and deep learning models. Large-scale language models trained on massive corpora can generate human-like text, answer questions, and perform translation tasks with remarkable accuracy. However, preprocessing remains a vital phase, as cleaning the text by converting it to lowercase, removing punctuation, and filtering out common stop words is essential to reduce noise. By refining the text data, machine learning algorithms can more easily identify patterns and learn semantic representations, making NLP a crucial component of data science applications that deal with text.
