Data science is an interdisciplinary field that combines scientific methods, processes, algorithms, and systems to extract knowledge and insights from structured and unstructured data. It unites concepts from statistics, mathematics, computer science, and domain expertise to solve complex data-driven problems. The lifecycle of a data science project typically begins with data collection and ingestion, followed by data cleaning, exploratory analysis, modeling, and communication of results.

A major portion of a data scientist's work involves data preprocessing, which includes handling missing values, filtering noise, and transforming variables. Once the data is prepared, data scientists use machine learning and statistical models to identify trends, make predictions, or discover hidden patterns. To store and retrieve these datasets, proficiency in database management and SQL is essential, allowing professionals to query structured databases efficiently. Additionally, data science relies on visualization tools to present complex findings to non-technical stakeholders in an understandable manner.

In recent years, the rise of big data and cloud computing has dramatically expanded the scale of data science. Tools for distributed computing allow data scientists to process terabytes of information across clusters of computers. Machine learning algorithms, particularly deep learning models, are often deployed in cloud environments to perform real-time predictions. Consequently, data science is closely connected to software engineering, as deploying predictive models into production requires writing robust, clean, and maintainable code.
