Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computer systems, networks, devices, and data from digital attacks, damage, or unauthorized access. In our increasingly interconnected world, where individuals, corporations, and governments rely heavily on digital systems, cybersecurity has become one of the most critical challenges of computer science. Security threats can come in various forms, including malware, ransomware, phishing, and denial-of-service attacks, each designed to exploit vulnerabilities in software or network configurations.

A robust cybersecurity strategy requires a multi-layered defense approach across computer networks, applications, and endpoints. To protect network traffic, security professionals implement firewalls, which monitor and filter incoming and outgoing network traffic based on security rules. Encryption is another fundamental technology, transforming sensitive data into unreadable ciphertext to prevent unauthorized access during transmission or storage. Access control mechanisms, such as multi-factor authentication, ensure that only authorized users can access sensitive systems and database management environments.

With the rapid adoption of cloud computing, the scope of cybersecurity has expanded significantly. Organizations must now secure virtual environments and shared cloud resources, ensuring that data stored in the cloud remains confidential and tamper-proof. Additionally, software engineering practices now emphasize 'security by design,' where vulnerability testing and threat modeling are integrated directly into the software development life cycle. Ultimately, cybersecurity is an ongoing battle that requires continuous monitoring, risk assessment, and user education to defend against evolving digital threats.
